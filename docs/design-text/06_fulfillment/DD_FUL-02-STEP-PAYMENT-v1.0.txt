 Computes the upfront amount the customer needs to pay, tells them how to pay it, and waits for the payment to arrive before
 letting the order advance. The "pay-first" gate. Phase: AWAITING_PAYMENT.



 1. What this step does
 In KE WIK, the operator policy is pay-first: the customer must pay the installation fee and first cycle prorate before any tech is
 dispatched. The order can't advance past this gate until the customer's payment lands in BIL-01.

 This step has three moving parts:

   1. Compute the expected amount (order-compute-expected-payment worker). The amount depends on the
     Package and the activation date. The worker resolves it by walking the catalogs: BIL-CFG-01 tells us which BillableEvents
     fire on ACTIVATION_INTENT (typically INSTALLATION_FEE + FIRST_CYCLE_PRORATE); each BillableEvent's
     service_refs[] resolves to one-shot Services in PLM-CFG-01 carrying prices; PLM-CFG-04 may apply discounts. The
     worker sums those into a single expectedPaymentAmount, writes it onto customer_order, sets a Camunda process
     variable, completes.

   2. Tell the customer how to pay (order-notify-payment-instructions worker). Emits the
     OrderAwaitingPayment Kafka event. DD_NOT-01 consumes that event and sends the customer an SMS+email with
     the paybill instructions (M-Pesa for WIK). The BPMN then parks at a message catch event named wait-payment — a
     BPMN step that sits and waits until someone notifies Camunda with messageName = PaymentReceived — guarded
     by a boundary timer (PT7D for WIK). The boundary timer is a side-step attached to the wait that fires after the configured
     duration if no message arrives — used to enforce a timeout. If the customer pays in time, the message fires and the
     BPMN advances. If the timer fires first, the order goes to STEP-CANCELLATION with reason PAYMENT_TIMEOUT.

   3. React to payment arriving (PaymentAppliedConsumer Kafka consumer). When the customer pays, the payment
     gateway adapter calls BIL-01's POST /api/payments/receive. BIL-01 allocates the payment (credit balance for
     POSTPAID customers in CREATED-subscription state, since no invoice exists yet) and emits
     sophix.billing.payment.applied. Our consumer picks up that event, looks up which order it's for, checks whether
     the cumulative amount is enough, and either notifies Camunda's wait-payment to wake up (full payment) or just
     updates the order with the partial amount (more is needed).

 Correlating payments back to orders
 The PaymentApplied event does NOT carry orderId directly — BIL-01 doesn't know about FUL-02's orders. It carries
 payment_reference (the gateway-generated reference, like mpesa_NK4XX7QP9) and targetReference (the reference
 the customer typed when paying, like the M-Pesa account number). Our consumer needs to map either of those back to an
 order.



SOPHIX BSS Design Documents                                                                                                                   Page 3
 The mechanism: when order-notify-payment-instructions tells the customer to pay, it tells them to type order_id
 as the M-Pesa account number (or equivalent for the operator's gateway). The customer types it; the gateway captures it as
 accountNumber; BIL-01 echoes it as targetReference in the PaymentApplied event payload. To make this lookup fast
 and explicit, the worker writes a side-channel row to customer_order_pending_payments keyed by
 payment_reference = order_id at notify time. PaymentAppliedConsumer then correlates via
 event.targetReference → customer_order_pending_payments.payment_reference → order_id.

 This side-channel exists because the alternative (looking up via subscription_id → invoice → payment) doesn't
 work in pay-first KE: at payment time, no invoice has been issued yet (the activation saga hasn't charged anything). The
 payment lands on the customer's credit balance, and there's no invoice-side handle. The side-channel keyed on order_id is
 the cleanest correlation.

 Partial payment
 Customers can split payments — pay 10000 KES today, 5000 KES tomorrow. Each PaymentApplied event is processed
 independently. The consumer accumulates the cumulative paid amount onto
 customer_order.payment_received_amount. If the cumulative is ≥ expected_payment_amount, the consumer fires
 PaymentReceived to Camunda; the BPMN advances. If it's below the threshold, the consumer emits a partial-payment
 Kafka event (consumed by DD_NOT-01 to tell the customer "we got X of Y; please pay the remaining Z") and the BPMN
 stays parked.

 What this step deliberately doesn't do
   - Doesn't charge the customer. Charging happens in STEP-ACTIVATION's activation saga, where BIL-01 charges the
     BillableEvents against the credit balance. This step just collects the upfront top-up. The actual amounts charged at
     activation may differ slightly (BIL-01 reapplies effective discounts, taxes, etc.).

   - Doesn't dispatch the customer payment via push. We tell them the paybill; they push the payment from their side
     (M-Pesa STK push is initiated by the customer, not us). For card-on-file scenarios (out of v1.0 scope), the model would be
     different.

   - Doesn't enforce currency conversion. The expected amount is in KES for KE WIK. Cross-currency is out of scope.



 2. Worked example end-to-end (WIK, single full payment)
 Continuing the John Mwangi scenario from STEP-CAPTURE and STEP-SUBSCRIPTION. Order
 ord_01HZX9K8M7Q2N4P6R3T5W8Y0Z1, customer cust_01HX3K9M2P5Q8R1T4W7Y0Z3N6, package
 pkg_INET_VOIP_RES, subscription sub_01HX5M2K9P3Q7R4T6W8Y0Z2N4Q. Camunda has just advanced past
 create-subscription to compute-expected-payment.

 Step 1 — Worker computes the expected amount
 ComputeExpectedPaymentWorker picks up the task. Process variables on the task: orderId, customerId,
 packageRef = pkg_INET_VOIP_RES, homepassId, operatorCode = WIK, subscriptionId = sub_01HX...A,
 intendedActivationDate = null (immediate).

 The worker walks the catalog to compute the activation cost:
          1. Fetch active BillableEvents for ACTIVATION_INTENT, operator WIK, package pkg_INET_VOIP_RES:
             → INSTALLATION_FEE (service_refs: [svc_installation_residential_gpon])
             → FIRST_CYCLE_PRORATE (service_refs: [svc_first_cycle_prorate])
               (filter: cycleAnchorOverflowApplied == PRORATE_PARTIAL_CYCLE — for now, immediate
                activation on day 18, anchor day TBD by SUB-WF-ACTIVATE-01; assume operator's default
                overflow policy resolves to anchor = today-day = 18, no proration needed)

          2. Resolve each one-shot Service's price from PLM-CFG-01:
             svc_installation_residential_gpon → price 5000.00 KES (catalog price)
             svc_first_cycle_prorate           → price computed from Package's monthly cycle price × prorate ratio
                                                 Package pkg_INET_VOIP_RES monthly price = 10000.00 KES
                                                 For immediate activation with anchor = today: full first cycle
                                                 → 10000.00 KES

          3. Apply PLM-CFG-04 discounts if any active for this Package + customer cohort:
             No active discounts for this customer → no adjustment.

          4. Sum: 5000.00 + 10000.00 = 15000.00 KES.

 The exact prorate logic depends on cycle_anchor_day and the operator's overflow policy, which is officially resolved by
 SUB-WF-ACTIVATE-01 at activation time (per R-SUB-LM-01-S-6a). STEP-PAYMENT uses a best-estimate prorate

 4. Worked example — payment timeout
 Customer fails to pay within PT7D.

 Variant — 7 days after step 2
 At 2026-05-25T09:00:03Z Camunda's job executor fires the boundary timer attached to wait-payment. The timer is
 interrupting (cancelActivity="true") — Camunda cancels the wait-payment activity and routes the flow to the
 timeout branch. The BPMN gateway sees the timeout-side path leads to order-cascade-cancellation with reason
 PAYMENT_TIMEOUT.

 The cascade worker runs (owned by STEP-CANCELLATION), checks what's set on the order:

   - subscription_id is set (sub_01HX5M2K9P3Q7R4T6W8Y0Z2N4Q) — calls SUB-WF-TERMINATE-01 to terminate the
     subscription.

   - work_order_id is NULL — no WO to cancel.

   - payment_received_amount = 0 — no refund needed.

 Cascade emits OrderCancellationCascadeCompleted (see STEP-CANCELLATION). Then order-mark-cancelled
 finalises:
            UPDATE customer_order
               SET current_phase   = 'CANCELLED',
                   failure_reason = 'PAYMENT_TIMEOUT',
                   failure_detail = 'Customer did not pay within PT7D of OrderAwaitingPayment',
                   last_updated_at = NOW()
             WHERE order_id        = 'ord_01HZX9K8M7Q2N4P6R3T5W8Y0Z1';

 Emits OrderCancelled (see STEP-CANCELLATION). DD_NOT-01 consumes it and sends John: "Your order was
 cancelled because payment was not received within 7 days. Please re-submit if you'd still like to subscribe."

 6. Message catch event
 6.1 PaymentReceived
 A message catch is a BPMN step that parks the process instance until someone notifies Camunda with a matching message
 name. Used here to wait for payment without polling.

 Triggering signal: our own PaymentAppliedConsumer calls Camunda's POST /engine-rest/message with
 messageName = PaymentReceived once the cumulative paid amount on the order reaches the threshold.

 Where BPMNs park: <bpmn:intermediateCatchEvent> named wait-payment in every operator BPMN that includes
 a pay-first phase.

 Boundary timer: interrupting timer attached. WIK PT7D. On fire → BPMN routes via timeout branch to
 order-cascade-cancellation with reason PAYMENT_TIMEOUT.

 Process variables set on correlation: paymentId, paidAmount, paymentMethod, paidCurrency (passed by
 PaymentAppliedConsumer so downstream BPMN steps can read them).

 9. Step-pinned rules
  ID                                                             Rule

  R-FUL-02-PAY-1                                                 expected_payment_amount is a snapshot computed at order-compute time.
                                                                 Not re-evaluated on partial-payment cycles. The actual charge during activation
                                                                 (BIL-01 against the customer's credit balance / wallet) may differ slightly due to
                                                                 operator overflow policy / discounts re-applied; differences are reconciled by
                                                                 BIL-01.

  R-FUL-02-PAY-2                                                 PaymentAppliedConsumer correlates via
                                                                 customer_order_pending_payments.payment_reference =
                                                                 event.targetReference (the customer typed order_id as the M-Pesa
                                                                 account number). The side-channel mapping table is written by
                                                                 order-notify-payment-instructions so the consumer has an explicit
                                                                 targetReference → order_id lookup independent of any invoice or
                                                                 subscription artifact (at payment time no invoice has been issued yet —
                                                                 payment lands on credit balance).

  R-FUL-02-PAY-3                                                 Partial-payment threshold: cumulative payment_received_amount ≥
                                                                 expected_payment_amount → correlate PaymentReceived and resolve
                                                                 the pending-payments row. Below → emit payment-received-partial and
                                                                 keep parking.

  R-FUL-02-PAY-4                                                 Payment timeout duration is BPMN-defined: KE WIK ships PT7D. On boundary
                                                                 timer fire → BPMN routes via interrupting boundary event to
                                                                 STEP-CANCELLATION's order-cascade-cancellation with reason
                                                                 PAYMENT_TIMEOUT. Timer is NOT reset by partial payments — its absolute
                                                                 deadline applies.

  R-FUL-02-PAY-5                                                 At-least-once Kafka delivery requires idempotency:
                                                                 customer_order_processed_payments UNIQUE on (order_id,
                                                                 payment_id). Re-processing of the same payment_id is skipped silently.

  R-FUL-02-PAY-6                                                 Admin manual offline payment via POST
                                                                 /api/admin/orders/{id}/mark-paid-offline (BIL-01 has its own
                                                                 admin endpoint for this; we route through BIL-01) routes through normal POST
                                                                 /api/payments/receive flow → emits PaymentApplied → our consumer
                                                                 correlates PaymentReceived. No direct /engine-rest/message bypass.

  R-FUL-02-PAY-7                                                 When PaymentAppliedConsumer cannot find a matching pending-payments
                                                                 row (consumer arrived after timer fired, order is CANCELLED): forward original
                                                                 event to sophix.orders.message-correlation-dlq. Finance reviews —
                                                                 may issue refund via BIL-02-ADJ-01.

  R-FUL-02-PAY-8                                                 Currency derived from operator default (WIK→KES, WUG→UGX, WTZ→TZS,
                                                                 YASSN→XOF). Cross-currency payment (customer pays in different currency
                                                                 than the order's expected_payment_currency) is rejected by BIL-01
                                                                 upstream of FUL-02; we never see it.

  R-FUL-02-PAY-9                                                 Zero-cost activation (no ACTIVATION_INTENT BillableEvents match the
                                                                 package) → expected_payment_amount = 0, partial-payment threshold
                                                                 check passes immediately, BPMN advances without waiting. Warning event
                                                                 emitted for ops verification (catalog inconsistency vs intentional free package).
